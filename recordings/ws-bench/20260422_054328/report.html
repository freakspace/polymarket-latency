Headline questions

Verdicts distilled from topology-level evidence in this run.

Q1 · No clear effect

Does warmup have any positive effect on data gaps and latency?

Only 0/4 topologies improved on both arrival and freshness after warmup, and only 1/4 improved on both miss rate and largest-gap size.

In this run, the immediate post-warmup window was not consistently cleaner than the warmup window.

Q2 · Mostly yes

Does more connections improve data quality, gaps, and latency?

2/3 larger topologies beat 1 ws on both coverage and miss rate, and 2/3 beat it on both gap runs and largest-gap size. 10 ws had the best coverage (100.0%), 10 ws had the lowest miss rate (0.0%), 10 ws had the best arrival P95 (40.7ms), and 5 ws had the best freshness P95 (655.9ms).

Latency was not monotonic: 3/3 larger topologies beat 1 ws on both arrival and freshness.

Q3 · Yes, at the pool level

Does more connections help prevent stalled connections (data gaps)?

Topology gap runs fell from 38 at 1 ws to 2 at 10 ws, and the largest topology-level gap fell from 263 to 36 events.

Redundancy did not stop sockets from stalling individually: 10/10 sockets in the 10-ws pool still had gap runs, and the worst single socket missed 665 events in one gap.

Caveats

  • Relative loss is topology-relative within this run, not authoritative venue loss.
  • Freshness depends on the venue timestamp carried by each websocket event.
  • Warmup is applied per connection and after reconnects; those observations are recorded but excluded from scored metrics.
  • Unique event aggregation uses a rolling 30.0s retention window to keep memory bounded on long runs.
  • Latency/freshness percentiles and histograms are estimated from bounded reservoir samples of up to 8192 values per metric.
